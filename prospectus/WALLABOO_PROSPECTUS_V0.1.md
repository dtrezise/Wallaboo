# MR WALLABOO GAMES

## Business Prospectus, Feasibility Study & Five-Year Plan

**Status:** WORKING — decision document, not a securities offering  
**Version:** v0.1  
**Prepared:** July 17, 2026  
**Founders / designers:** Dan Trezise and Micah Trezise  
**Planning horizon:** 2027–2031

> **Recommendation: CONDITIONAL GO.** The opportunity is credible as a lean, milestone-gated independent publisher. The company is not ready for a manufacturing commitment, crowdfunding launch, or full-time founder payroll. The next investment should buy evidence: one validated game, a cleared brand, a qualified audience, and firm supplier/fulfillment quotes.

## 1. Executive Summary

Mr Wallaboo Games is a proposed independent tabletop-game studio and publisher built around original games designed by Dan Trezise and Micah Trezise, individually and together. The recovered strategy source deliberately leaves the brand personality, target audience, product mix, publishing model, and manufacturing strategy open. The only named game concept recovered from the shared conversation is *The Republic*, a game about rising to power in 1930s Germany; its separate design dossier was not publicly recoverable.

The commercial opportunity is real but crowded. The U.S. Games/Puzzles supercategory reached $4.9 billion in 2025, and Kickstarter reported $220 million pledged to successful tabletop campaigns in 2024. Those totals overstate the specific opportunity for a new original-game publisher: Pokémon and trading cards drove much of recent market growth, trading-card games represent roughly two-thirds of hobby-game dollars, and thousands of tabletop campaigns compete for attention. [S01–S06]

The recommended entry strategy is therefore narrow:

1. Develop one approachable, paper-heavy flagship game with a $29–$39 target MSRP, 20–45 minute playtime, two-to-five-player reach, and no custom molds.
2. Use public playtesting and a print-and-play prototype to establish demand before expensive art or tooling.
3. Launch primarily through crowdfunding/direct sales, but build retail-compatible unit economics from the first bill of materials.
4. Keep *The Republic* as a separate adult historical-strategy track until its design, historical framing, content policy, and retailer/platform fit are independently validated.
5. Treat the company as a disciplined side-stage venture until the catalog supports repeat demand and founder compensation.

Under the illustrative base case, net revenue grows from approximately $67,000 in 2027 to $688,000 in 2031. EBITDA after modest founder compensation turns positive in 2030 and reaches approximately $112,000 in 2031. The modeled $75,000 seed reserve covers the base-case operating trough, but the conservative case consumes approximately $158,000 over five years. The upside case turns positive in year two. These outcomes are highly sensitive to unit volume, direct-to-consumer mix, landed cost, and title quality.

## 4. Company and Brand Thesis

### 4.1 Proposed mission

Create original tabletop games that reward smart choices, invite repeat play, and feel distinctive without imposing unnecessary rules or component complexity.

This mission translates the working source's example pillars—accessibility, memorable worlds, replayability, table presence, and elegant mechanics—into a testable direction. It does not lock those examples as canon.

### 4.2 Brand promise

**Easy to invite. Worth replaying. Distinctive on the table.**

Every release should pass four filters:

- A new player can understand why the game is fun within five minutes.
- The first play completes within the advertised time range.
- Player choices produce a reason to play again.
- The product can be explained in one sentence and recognized across a room.

### 4.3 Portfolio architecture

Use one master brand and two internal development lanes, not two consumer-facing subbrands at launch:

- **Accessible lane:** compact party, family, and gateway-strategy games; the recommended launch lane.
- **Deep-strategy lane:** longer, more demanding, or historically sensitive designs; *The Republic* belongs here unless testing proves otherwise.

Consumer-facing subbrands should be created only after two successful releases establish a real need for navigation.

## 5. Product Strategy

### 5.1 Recommended flagship specification

| Attribute | Launch target | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Format | Cards, punchboard, stock dice/pawns, compact box | Keeps MOQ and tooling risk manageable [S09–S12] |
| MSRP | $29–$39 | Supports DTC value while leaving a plausible wholesale margin |
| Players | 2–5 | Covers couples, families, and small game groups |
| Playtime | 20–45 minutes | Easier demonstrations, reviews, and repeat sessions |
| Age position | 10+/12+ only if compliance plan supports it; otherwise design and market honestly for 14+ | Age grading affects CPSC obligations [S14–S15] |
| Complexity | Gateway to light-medium | Matches the proposed brand promise and widens test recruitment |
| Differentiator | One visible mechanical hook plus one memorable world/character hook | Improves explainability and shelf/campaign communication |
| Production | No bespoke plastic molds in the first edition | Avoids MOQ and capital escalation [S12] |

### 5.2 Stage gates

1. **Concept gate:** one-sentence hook, audience, playtime, and target cost approved by both founders.
2. **Prototype gate:** complete game loop works with inexpensive components; ten internal sessions logged.
3. **Playtest gate:** at least 30 observed external sessions across at least six groups; issues tracked by severity.
4. **Blind-test gate:** at least ten groups can learn from the rulebook without a founder; at least 80% finish within the stated time; no critical rules ambiguity remains.
5. **Commercial gate:** two comparable manufacturing quotes, fulfillment estimate, compliance classification, MSRP/wholesale margin check, and prelaunch demand threshold met.
6. **Manufacturing gate:** final files, signed approvals, quality-control plan, cash reserve, and contingency approved.

These are management thresholds, not industry statistics.

### 5.3 The Republic

*The Republic* may become a distinctive adult historical-strategy title, but it should not carry the company's first-launch burden without evidence. Its premise creates four additional risks: longer development, historical accuracy and framing, platform/advertising sensitivity, and retailer/customer hesitation around extremist imagery or role-play. The project should remain `WORKING` and be evaluated through a separate design-and-content review. No unrecovered mechanics are assumed in this prospectus.

## 6. Market Research

### 6.1 Market size with the right caveat

The broad U.S. toy market generated $30.3 billion in tracked retail sales in 2025. Games/Puzzles was the largest supercategory at $4.9 billion, up 37%. Circana attributes the category surge primarily to Pokémon, so the number should be treated as an upper context boundary—not a serviceable market for an unknown original board game. [S01–S02]

ICv2 estimated the U.S./Canada hobby-games market at $3.66 billion in 2025, up 40%, but reported that trading-card games account for about two-thirds of hobby-game dollars while the other categories were flat or showed modest growth. Board-game publishers also faced tariff disruption and an unusually heavy flow of product. [S03]

The investable insight is not “the category is booming.” It is that consumers still spend heavily on social, collectible, and repeat-play products, but original board games must win attention one title at a time.

### 6.2 Crowdfunding

Kickstarter reported 6,646 tabletop projects launched in 2024, 5,314 funded, and $220 million pledged to successful tabletop campaigns. The 80% platform success rate includes established publishers, low-goal projects, RPGs, expansions, and small items; it must not be used as Wallaboo's probability of success. In 2025, tabletop project count rose while overall Kickstarter dollars declined, and Gamefound/BackerKit continued to compete for tabletop projects. [S05–S06]

Kickstarter charges a 5% platform fee and approximately 3%–5% payment processing. The financial model uses 9% combined. Shipping charged through a campaign can inflate the funding total and fee base; the campaign budget should separate product revenue, collected shipping, taxes, and fulfillment liabilities. [S07–S08]

### 6.3 Customer segments

| Segment | Need | Wallaboo entry message | Primary channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social families and mixed-experience groups | Fast teaching, reliable duration, replay value | “A smart game everyone can join” | DTC, gift, specialty retail |
| Gateway hobby gamers | A distinctive hook without a rules wall | “Interesting choices in under 45 minutes” | Kickstarter, reviewers, hobby stores |
| Collectors / super-fans | Creator story, limited extras, early access | “Founders' edition without gameplay fragmentation” | Crowdfunding, email, conventions |
| Deep-strategy audience | Historical or systems depth | Separate positioning for *The Republic* and later designs | Hobby media, conventions, specialist crowdfunding |

### 6.4 Competitive reference models

- **Focused micro-publisher:** Button Shy demonstrates how a strict physical format and frequent small releases can create identity and lower product risk. Wallaboo should borrow the focus, not the monthly cadence. [S21]
- **Distinctive strategy studio:** Leder Games combines a recognizable design point of view, replayability, and holistic product/art development. Wallaboo cannot match its catalog initially but can emulate the discipline of one clear mechanical identity per title. [S22]
- **Premium catalog publisher:** Stonemaier illustrates a broad price ladder, DTC infrastructure, expansions, and accessories. That is a mature-state reference, not a launch template. [S23]

Wallaboo's defendable position is not “another indie publisher.” It must become a trusted curator of a specific play experience. The proposed first position—approachable, clever, replayable, visually distinctive—is promising but still too broad until one product proves it.

## 7. Go-to-Market Plan

### 7.1 Channel sequence

1. **Audience development:** development diary, print-and-play signups, local/public tests, designer notes, and email capture.
2. **Proof campaign:** Kickstarter or comparable platform after audience gates; one core pledge, one premium bundle, limited add-ons.
3. **DTC continuity:** own email list and webstore for late pledges, replacement parts, and reorders.
4. **Retail-direct pilots:** 20–40 stores with low case packs, demo support, and reorder tracking.
5. **Distribution:** only after demonstrated sell-through and a landed cost that works at approximately 40% of MSRP. [S13]

### 7.2 Prelaunch metrics and gates

Do not launch based on follower count alone. Require:

- 1,500 qualified prelaunch followers across owned email and platform followers.
- At least 600 opted-in email subscribers with tracked source and engagement.
- At least 150 completed public playtest sessions across the development period.
- At least 100 written intent-to-back responses or refundable reservations where lawful and platform-compliant.
- A day-one support plan representing at least 30% of the campaign goal.
- Two independent reviews/previews scheduled, with no paid-review influence over conclusions.

These are internal risk gates. They are intentionally stricter than simply opening a campaign page.

### 7.3 Marketing system

Use a founder-led content engine: one meaningful development update per week, one playtest/reviewer outreach batch per month, and quarterly public prototype milestones. Paid media should amplify proven creative, not discover the product's audience from scratch. The email list is the durable asset; platform followers are rented reach.

## 8. Operating and Manufacturing Plan

### 8.1 Build-versus-partner recommendation

Use a hybrid publishing model:

- Retain game IP, creative direction, brand, community, and commercial decisions.
- Contract specialist development/editing, illustration, graphic design, manufacturing, freight, fulfillment, accounting, and legal work.
- Do not hire fixed full-time staff until the catalog produces repeatable contribution margin.

### 8.2 Manufacturing economics

Public 2026 budgetary data indicates that a straightforward folding-board/card/token game may cost $8–$15 per unit at 1,500+ copies; one published sample was $10.60 at 1,000 copies and $14.28 at 500. PrintNinja's minimum is 500 games and Panda's is 1,500. A typical production-plus-ocean timeline can be 10–14 weeks after proof approval, before contingency. [S09–S11]

The base model begins at a $12.50 landed unit cost and declines to $9.25 as volume rises. “Landed” includes manufacturing, ordinary freight, duty/tariff reserve, inbound handling, and quality loss. This is a model assumption, not a quote.

### 8.3 Import policy

The U.S. imposed a temporary 10% import surcharge through July 24, 2026 unless modified or extended, while China-specific arrangements and legal authorities have changed repeatedly. The company should never price a campaign using a stale tariff snapshot. Every production approval needs a current customs classification, country-of-origin analysis, and downside quote. [S19–S20]

### 8.4 Quality and fulfillment

- Approve white sample, digital proof, and pre-production copy before mass production.
- Define acceptable-quality limits for color, registration, component counts, warping, and packaging damage.
- Hold 2%–3% replacement stock.
- Collect shipping after the campaign only if the pledge-manager economics and customer communication are explicit.
- Do not subsidize worldwide shipping without lane-level quotes.
- Require fulfillment data ownership, export access, and service-level reporting.

## 9. Regulatory, Legal, and Governance

### 9.1 Product classification

CPSC says traditional board/table games that appeal equally to children and adults may be general-use products; child-directed features, junior positioning, age, small parts, and marketing can change the classification. Toys intended primarily for children 12 and under generally require third-party testing and a Children's Product Certificate. [S14–S15]

Age grading must follow actual design and marketing, not be used as a label-only shortcut. Obtain a written compliance plan before final packaging.

### 9.2 Intellectual property

Before public launch:

- Complete professional federal, state, domain, marketplace, and common-law clearance for MR WALLABOO and WALLABOO-related marks.
- Resolve the existing Dutch WALLABOO brand finding with counsel; different goods do not eliminate all risk.
- Assign all founder-created game, art, text, and brand IP to the company under written agreements.
- Use contractor work-made-for-hire and assignment language.
- File only after the goods/services strategy is clear; the current USPTO base fee is $350 per class before legal and additional fees. [S16–S18]

### 9.3 Founder governance

Execute an operating/founders agreement before outside spend. It should address equity, cash contributions, time commitments, design credits, solo versus joint IP, approval thresholds, deadlock, departure, incapacity, expense authority, and treatment of archived concepts. A two-founder company needs a deadlock mechanism before it needs a logo.

Proposed operating roles, subject to founder agreement:

- **Creative/Product Lead:** game vision, prototype ownership, playtest synthesis, design sign-off.
- **Publishing/Operations Lead:** budget, schedules, suppliers, campaign, fulfillment, compliance coordination.
- **Joint reserved matters:** product greenlight, print quantity, debt/equity, IP sale/license, founder compensation, and brand changes.

### 11.4 Unit-economics discipline

Traditional distribution may yield only about 40% of MSRP to the publisher, versus approximately 50% in retail-direct arrangements and close to full MSRP before fees in DTC. [S13] A $39 game with a $12.50 landed cost can work in DTC but becomes thin through distribution. The commercial gate should require:

- landed cost no more than 25% of MSRP for a distribution-intended edition, ideally lower;
- DTC contribution margin above 45% after platform fees, refund reserve, and fulfillment handling;
- distributor-channel contribution above 15% before fixed overhead;
- no campaign stretch goal that makes the retail edition uneconomic.

### 11.5 Break-even

At base launch economics, the weighted contribution is roughly $15 per unit. Cash fixed costs before founder compensation are approximately $54,000, implying about 3,500 units for year-one operating break-even. The base plan sells 2,000 units, so a planned first-year loss is expected. By 2031, scale and cost reduction lower the all-in annual break-even to roughly 18,000 units including modeled founder compensation, compared with 26,000 base-case units.

## 13. 24-Month Execution Roadmap

### Phase 0 — Formation and clearance (0–30 days)

- Execute founder/operating and IP-assignment agreements.
- Run professional trademark clearance and reserve viable domains/social handles.
- Select the flagship from a scored shortlist; define target customer, promise, and component ceiling.
- Set repository governance, decision log, and monthly budget.

**Exit gate:** company/IP ownership is unambiguous and the working brand is commercially clear enough to test.

### Phase 1 — Prototype and problem/solution proof (months 1–4)

- Build a low-cost physical and print-and-play prototype.
- Log at least ten internal and 30 external sessions.
- Publish development notes and begin email capture.
- Test the one-sentence pitch, teach time, game duration, and desire to replay.

**Exit gate:** external players request another play and the design meets the target experience.

### Phase 2 — Blind testing and commercial design (months 4–8)

- Run at least ten blind-test groups.
- Lock rules architecture and preliminary bill of materials.
- Obtain two manufacturer estimates and one domestic/small-run comparison.
- Obtain fulfillment, product-safety, and insurance guidance.

**Exit gate:** rulebook works without founders; target MSRP supports all three channels.

### Phase 3 — Audience and campaign proof (months 7–12)

- Commission final art/graphic work in stages.
- Build prelaunch page, email sequence, preview prototypes, and campaign budget.
- Reach audience thresholds and document day-one commitments.
- Requote manufacturing, duty, freight, and fulfillment immediately before launch.

**Exit gate:** $60,000 product-pledge goal is supported by evidence, not hope.

### Phase 4 — Campaign and production (months 12–18)

- Run a focused campaign with limited SKUs.
- Finalize quantity only after collected funds and late-pledge assumptions are reconciled.
- Approve samples, testing, packaging, and quality controls.
- Communicate monthly through fulfillment.

### Phase 5 — Delivery, retail proof, and catalog decision (months 18–24)

- Deliver, resolve replacements, and publish a fulfillment postmortem.
- Pilot retail-direct accounts and track sell-through/reorders.
- Measure review sentiment, repeat demand, list retention, and contribution margin.
- Greenlight title two, license the design, or pause based on evidence.

## 14. Decision Framework

### Approve the next phase only if

- the preceding gate is documented;
- committed spend fits within the remaining reserve;
- current quotes preserve target contribution margin;
- both founders approve reserved matters; and
- no unresolved critical legal, compliance, or fulfillment issue remains.

### Stop, license, or redesign if

- blind testers cannot learn the game reliably;
- prelaunch demand cannot support the true funding goal;
- landed cost exceeds retail-compatible economics;
- the brand cannot be cleared at reasonable cost; or
- founder time commitments cannot sustain delivery.

## 15. Final Recommendation

Authorize a six-month, capped validation program—not a full company launch. The objective is to convert Mr Wallaboo from a broad creative intention into one commercially testable promise. Spend first on prototype quality, testing, ownership, clearance, and audience evidence. Defer deluxe components, speculative inventory, payroll expansion, and *The Republic* production until the flagship proves that the company can create, sell, and deliver one game well.

If the commercial gate is reached, proceed with a focused crowdfunding/DTC launch and preserve retail compatibility. If it is not reached, the company will have learned at low cost and can redesign, license, or stop without an inventory crisis.
